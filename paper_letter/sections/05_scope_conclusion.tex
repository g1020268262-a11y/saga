\section{Scope and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Our analysis proceeds from a formal authorization-coverage diagnostic to a
concrete implementation defect and then to its authorization-bypass
consequences. The diagnostic shows that the existing authentication and
token-secrecy results do not establish the required policy-authorization
obligation. Implementation inspection then identifies a production matcher
violation of the documented most-specific semantics. In controlled executions,
the resulting decision reaches token issuance and storage; under the
corresponding fixed symbolic divergence, \texttt{ChatAccept} is reachable even
though its prior-\texttt{TokenIssue} correspondence remains true. Token-origin
consistency can therefore coexist with specification denial.

The evidence covers one recorded SAGA case and the tested policies. It does not
establish arbitrary-policy correctness, Python--ProVerif refinement, an
arbitrary-attacker exploit, or a deployed exploit. Empirical execution stops
before application-message acceptance; that endpoint belongs only to the
symbolic model.
