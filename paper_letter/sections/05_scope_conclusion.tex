\section{Scope and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For the recorded SAGA policy, order-dependent matching authorizes an agent
denied by the intended most-specific semantics. The resulting permission
reaches token issuance and storage through controlled production consumers;
the corresponding fixed symbolic model permits \texttt{ChatAccept} while its
prior-\texttt{TokenIssue} correspondence holds. Authentication and token-origin
consistency are insufficient to establish compliance with the receiver's
intended authorization policy.

These findings concern the recorded case, tested policies, fixtures, and
symbolic assumptions. They establish neither arbitrary-policy correctness nor
Python-to-ProVerif refinement or an arbitrary-attacker or deployed exploit.
Consumer execution stops at token issuance and storage; message acceptance
belongs only to the symbolic model.
