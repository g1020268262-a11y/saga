\section{Authorization Decision Inconsistency}
\label{sec:authorization-inconsistency}

\paragraph{Workflow and policy semantics.}
An initiator requests contact through SAGA's Provider. The receiver's ordered
policy maps identity patterns to integer budgets. Provider and receiver
independently call the production matcher on the initiator. The Provider gates
one-time key (OTK) release; the receiver rechecks its policy before token issuance.

For a fixed policy and valid identity, SAGA v2, Section~IV-D.1~\cite{syros2025saga},
specifies that the most-specific matching rule is selected; the implementation
scores patterns with \texttt{aid\_specificity()}.
A positive budget permits the request; a negative budget denies it.
We do not consider equal-specificity conflicts, policy changes during
evaluation, or zero budgets.

\paragraph{Root cause and observation.}
The tested production matcher is \texttt{match()} in
\texttt{saga/common/\allowbreak contact\_policy.py}, unchanged from upstream
SAGA revision \texttt{7372111}, also the matcher-test baseline in our study
copy.\footnote{Upstream: \texttt{github.com/gsiros/saga}; study copy and artifacts:
\texttt{github.com/g1020268262-a11y/saga}.}
Its \texttt{best\_pattern} starts at \texttt{None}, whose specificity is $-1$.
A matching rule with greater specificity updates the budget but never
\texttt{best\_pattern}. Thus later matching rules still compare against $-1$,
allowing even a less-specific wildcard to overwrite the budget.
In the recorded case, \texttt{aid\_specificity()} scores
\texttt{alice@example.com:*} as $70$
and \texttt{*} as $0$; their budgets are $-1$ and $10$, respectively.

An independent finite specification evaluator selects the specific denial
for both orders in Table~\ref{tab:matcher-evaluation}. When specific deny
precedes wildcard allow, the specification denies with budget $-1$, but the
production matcher returns $10$ and permits. The reverse order returns the
specified denial, establishing order dependence. These calls establish only
a local decision inconsistency, not a deployed exploit, message
acceptance, or whole-system failure.
