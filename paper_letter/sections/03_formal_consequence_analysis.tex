\section{Formal Authorization Analysis}
\label{sec:formalization}

\paragraph{Coverage diagnostic.}
The archived ProVerif 2.05 diagnostic extends SAGA's reference model. It
preserves the original authentication and token-secrecy results but reports
an added correspondence requiring prior \texttt{PolicyAllow} for
\texttt{Accept} as false. \texttt{Accept} denotes token receipt and
verification, distinct from
\texttt{ChatAccept}. \texttt{PolicyAllow} is not connected to policy
enforcement; this is an authorization proof-coverage gap. The diagnostic
neither executes the Python matcher nor identifies its defect.

\paragraph{Model and assumptions.}
\label{sec:implementation-mapping}
The Decision Bridge transfers the checked specification-deny/implementation-allow
decision pair into a fixed symbolic scenario. ProVerif assumes this pair; it
does not execute Python or the production matcher or discover the defect.
The Bridge is neither a general code-to-model translator nor a proof of
Python-to-ProVerif refinement. The executions are compared only through
authorization decisions and token-lifecycle events.

The initiator has a valid registered identity, follows the prescribed symbolic
peer process, and cannot alter the receiver's fixed policy. Reachability is
therefore not an arbitrary-adversary claim. The Provider and Certificate
Authority (CA) are trusted, cryptographic checks are effective, and private keys
are uncompromised. A private symbolic channel abstracts authenticated,
confidential transport.

In the symbolic receiver, \texttt{TokenIssue}$(R,I,t)$ follows \texttt{ActiveToken}
storage of receiver $R$, initiator $I$, encrypted token $t$, and $I$'s public
access-control key, and precedes transmission. For private-channel message
$m$, \texttt{ChatAccept}$(R,I,t,m)$ occurs only after checking the fixed identity
and an \texttt{ActiveToken} entry matching $R$, $I$, $t$, and $I$'s sender public key.

\paragraph{Authorization conditions and results.}
The artifact archives the ProVerif 2.05 model and raw output. The run reports
\texttt{ChatAccept} reachable under the fixed divergence; separately, it
verifies the non-injective token-origin correspondence:
\[
 \mathsf{ChatAccept}(R,I,t,m)
 \Longrightarrow \mathsf{TokenIssue}(R,I,t)
\]
Here $R$ is the receiver and token issuer, and $I$ is the initiator. Prior
issuance must match $R$, $I$, and encrypted token $t$. For a static receiver
policy, a distinct ideal authorization condition is
\[
 \mathsf{TokenIssue}(R,I,t)
 \Longrightarrow \mathsf{Allow}_{\mathrm{spec}}(P_R,I),
\]
where $P_R$ is $R$'s contact policy and \(\mathsf{Allow}_{\mathrm{spec}}\)
denotes permission under its intended semantics, rather than the matcher
result. This second formula is a desired condition, neither an executed
ProVerif query nor a proved theorem.

For the recorded specific-deny then wildcard-allow case, the correspondence
coexists with intended-policy denial. Token-origin consistency therefore does
not imply policy-compliant issuance. The \texttt{ChatAccept} witness includes
issuance; no standalone \texttt{TokenIssue} reachability query exists.
