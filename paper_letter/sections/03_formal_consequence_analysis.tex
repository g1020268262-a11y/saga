\section{Formal Consequence Analysis}
\label{sec:formalization}
\label{sec:attack-path}

\paragraph{Decision input.}
\label{sec:implementation-mapping}
Independent specification evaluation and the matcher observation supply
the fixed divergence
\[
 (D_{\mathrm{spec}}(P,I),D_{\mathrm{impl}}^{\mathrm{obs}}(P,I))
       =(\mathsf{Deny},\mathsf{Allow}).
\]
The decision bridge reconstructs the fixed symbolic scenario from checked
evidence. ProVerif consumes these fixed facts; it neither executes Python
nor discovers the matcher inconsistency, and no implementation refinement
is claimed.

\paragraph{Model assumptions.}
The initiator has a genuine registered identity and cannot change the
receiver's fixed policy. The Provider, CA, cryptographic checks, and honest
private keys are trusted. Symbolic processes represent material release,
token issuance, and application-message acceptance; a private channel and
shared tables abstract authenticated transport, handoff, and active-token
state.

\paragraph{Queries and results.}
The ProVerif~2.05 run reports \texttt{ChatAccept} reachable under the fixed
divergence and establishes the non-injective correspondence
\[
 \mathsf{ChatAccept}(R,I,t,m)
 \Longrightarrow \mathsf{TokenIssue}(R,I,t)
\]
as true. The correspondence requires prior issuance for the same participants
and token; it does not establish policy permission. Token issuance appears
in the acceptance witness, but there is no standalone \texttt{TokenIssue}
reachability query.

The formal execution and consumer validation are separate experiments:
the former reaches symbolic message acceptance, while the latter stops at
token issuance and storage.
