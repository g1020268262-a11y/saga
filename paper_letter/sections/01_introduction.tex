\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

LLM-based agents increasingly use external tools and
services~\cite{wang2024agents,debenedetti2024agentdojo}.
Authentication establishes identity; receiver-side authorization determines
whether the identified agent may communicate~\cite{ellison1999spki}.
SAGA~\cite{syros2025saga} combines authenticated identities, contact policies,
and access-control tokens. Its existing authentication and token-secrecy
results do not establish that token issuance follows the receiver's intended
policy.

An authorization coverage diagnostic exposes this proof-coverage gap. It
motivates code inspection and controlled matcher tests, which reveal an
order-dependent decision inconsistency. We examine its consumer propagation
and consequences in a fixed symbolic scenario.

Our contributions are (1) an authorization proof-coverage gap in SAGA's
reference model; (2) an order-dependent production matcher inconsistency and
controlled consumer propagation to token issuance and storage; and (3) formal
consequence analysis showing symbolic \texttt{ChatAccept} reachability with a
true prior-\texttt{TokenIssue} correspondence under a fixed
specification-deny/implementation-allow divergence.

\paragraph{Related work.}
\label{sec:related-work}
Prior work studies authorization languages and policy
semantics~\cite{becker2004cassandra,becker2007secpal} and
policy verification and change-impact analysis~\cite{fisler2005margrave}.
SPKI~\cite{ellison1999spki} and Macaroons~\cite{birgisson2014macaroons}
provide credential- and capability-based authorization abstractions.
ProVerif and Tamarin support symbolic protocol
verification~\cite{blanchet2016proverif,meier2013tamarin}.
We complement SAGA's protocol guarantees by examining a concrete
policy-evaluation error and its consumer consequences under SAGA's model
assumptions.
