\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

SAGA~\cite{syros2025saga} uses contact policies and access-control tokens
to authorize communication between registered agents. Authentication
establishes identity but does not imply permission under the receiver's
contact policy.

We identify an order-dependent inconsistency in the production matcher.
Under documented most-specific semantics, a specific deny should override
a wildcard allow. Instead, the matcher returns the later wildcard allowance;
reversing the rule order returns denial.

We examine two consequences. Controlled production consumers propagate
the inconsistent decision through the Provider and receiver to token
issuance/storage. Separately, a symbolic model instantiated with the same
fixed divergence reaches \texttt{ChatAccept}; ProVerif also reports its
correspondence to prior \texttt{TokenIssue} as true. The empirical execution
stops at token storage, before message acceptance.

Our contributions are (1) root-cause analysis of the recorded matcher
inconsistency, (2) controlled validation of its propagation to token
issuance/storage, and (3) conditional formal analysis of symbolic message
acceptance and prior issuance.

\paragraph{Related work.}
\label{sec:related-work}
Authorization-policy work studies policy semantics and
analysis~\cite{becker2007secpal,fisler2005margrave}; ProVerif supports
symbolic protocol analysis~\cite{blanchet2016proverif}. We relate a concrete
matcher disagreement to both forms of analysis.
