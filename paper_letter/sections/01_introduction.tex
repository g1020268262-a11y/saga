\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

SAGA~\cite{syros2025saga} governs communication between registered agents
using contact policies, identity checks, and access-control tokens.
Authentication establishes identity, not compliance with the receiver's
contact policy.

Direct matcher calls show order dependence: specific denial followed by
wildcard permission returns permission, whereas the reverse order returns
denial; most-specific semantics deny both. For the fixed specification-deny/
implementation-allow case, ProVerif reports \texttt{ChatAccept} reachable and
its correspondence to prior \texttt{TokenIssue} true. A separate controlled
production-consumer execution reaches token issuance/storage. The formal and
empirical endpoints are symbolic message acceptance and token storage,
respectively.

Our contributions are (1) analysis of the recorded matcher inconsistency,
(2) consumer-side propagation validation with A/A, D/D, and D/A controls,
and (3) conditional formal analysis of reachability and prior issuance.

\paragraph{Related work.}
\label{sec:related-work}
Authorization-policy work studies policy semantics and
analysis~\cite{becker2007secpal,fisler2005margrave}; ProVerif supports
symbolic protocol analysis~\cite{blanchet2016proverif}. We relate a concrete
matcher disagreement to both forms of analysis.
