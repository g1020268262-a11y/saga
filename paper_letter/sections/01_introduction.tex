\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

LLM-based agents increasingly use external tools and
services~\cite{wang2024agents,debenedetti2024agentdojo}.
Authentication establishes identity, whereas receiver-side
authorization determines whether the identified agent may
communicate~\cite{ellison1999spki}. Authentication and token
secrecy alone do not establish compliance with the receiver's
intended contact policy.

SAGA~\cite{syros2025saga} governs communication between
registered agents using contact policies and access-control
tokens. Its policy matcher implements the receiver's
authorization decision. We investigate:
\emph{Can a mismatch between the receiver's intended policy
and its implementation permit a denied agent, and how far
can that permission propagate through the protocol?}

We identify an order-dependent matcher defect that violates
SAGA's most-specific-rule semantics, yielding specification
denial but implementation permission. An authorization
coverage diagnostic identifies a missing proof obligation
without discovering the implementation defect. Controlled
executions of production Provider and receiver code paths
confirm propagation to token issuance and storage.
Under the corresponding evidence-linked fixed symbolic
divergence, ProVerif reports \texttt{ChatAccept} reachable
while the prior-\texttt{TokenIssue} correspondence holds.
Actual application-message acceptance is not established.

Our contributions are (1) identifying and explaining a
concrete authorization decision inconsistency in SAGA;
(2) analyzing its conditional symbolic consequences using
ProVerif; and (3) validating its propagation to token
issuance and storage through controlled production-code
execution.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Prior work examines authorization languages and
semantics~\cite{becker2004cassandra,becker2007secpal},
and policy verification~\cite{fisler2005margrave}.
SPKI~\cite{ellison1999spki} and
Macaroons~\cite{birgisson2014macaroons} provide related
authorization abstractions. ProVerif and Tamarin support
symbolic protocol analysis~\cite{blanchet2016proverif,meier2013tamarin}.
Our analysis connects a concrete policy-implementation
inconsistency to its consumer and symbolic consequences.
