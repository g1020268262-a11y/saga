\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

SAGA~\cite{syros2025saga} uses contact policies and access-control tokens
to authorize communication between registered agents. Authentication
establishes identity but does not imply permission under the receiver's
contact policy.

We identify an order-dependent inconsistency in the production matcher.
Under documented most-specific semantics, a specific deny should override
a wildcard allow. Instead, the matcher returns the later wildcard allowance;
reversing the rule order returns denial.

Controlled production consumers propagate the decision to token issuance/storage,
stopping before message acceptance. A separate symbolic model analyzes message
acceptance under the supplied divergence.

Our contributions are (1) identifying and explaining the matcher inconsistency,
and (2) characterizing its consequences through controlled production-consumer
propagation and conditional symbolic analysis.

\paragraph{Related work.}
\label{sec:related-work}
Prior work studies policy-language semantics~\cite{becker2007secpal} and
policy verification/change impact~\cite{fisler2005margrave}.
SAGA itself analyzes protocol properties~\cite{syros2025saga} using
ProVerif~\cite{blanchet2016proverif}. We examine a concrete implementation's
deviation from specified rule selection and its consumer effects; this does
not refute SAGA's guarantees under its model assumptions.
