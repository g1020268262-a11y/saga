\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

SAGA~\cite{syros2025saga} governs communication between registered agents
using contact policies, identity checks, and access-control tokens.
Authentication establishes who is communicating; it does not establish
whether the receiving user's policy permits that contact. We present a
formal and empirical analysis of a recorded authorization decision
inconsistency in SAGA.

Direct calls to the unmodified matcher show that a specific denial followed
by wildcard permission returns an allow-side budget, whereas reversing the
same rules returns denial. Documented most-specific semantics require denial
in both orders. We analyze the conditional consequences of this disagreement
in a fixed symbolic scenario: ProVerif reports message acceptance reachable
and establishes that acceptance implies prior token issuance. Separately,
controlled consumer experiments execute the production Provider and receiver
through token issuance and storage, stopping before message acceptance.
These are distinct formal and empirical results.

Our case-study contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Recorded decision inconsistency analysis.}
    We characterize the recorded disagreement through source inspection,
    direct matcher calls, and independent finite specification evaluation.
  \item \textbf{Consumer-side propagation validation.}
    A/A, D/D, and D/A specification/implementation controls connect fresh
    matcher observations to the production authorization workflow through
    token issuance and storage in a controlled environment.
  \item \textbf{Conditional formal consequence analysis.}
    We analyze reachability and prior-issuance correspondence under explicit
    symbolic assumptions, with independent gate controls distinguishing
    modeled release and issuance boundaries.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Related work.}
\label{sec:related-work}
Agent research addresses autonomous behavior, trust, and prompt
injection~\cite{wang2024agents,ramchurn2004trust,debenedetti2024agentdojo}.
Our focus is policy evaluation and its consumers. Authentication and
distributed authority have longstanding foundations~\cite{lampson1992authentication};
SPKI, OAuth, and Macaroons address credentials and
delegation~\cite{ellison1999spki,hardt2012oauth,birgisson2014macaroons}.
Cassandra, SecPAL, and Margrave address policy semantics and
analysis~\cite{becker2004cassandra,becker2007secpal,fisler2005margrave},
while ProVerif and Tamarin support symbolic protocol
analysis~\cite{blanchet2016proverif,meier2013tamarin}.
This study concerns a concrete implementation disagreement and its
conditional consequences, rather than a new policy language or general
verification method.
