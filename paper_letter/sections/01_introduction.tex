\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

LLM-based autonomous agents increasingly operate with external tools and
services~\cite{wang2024agents,debenedetti2024agentdojo}.
SAGA~\cite{syros2025saga} uses contact policies and access-control tokens
to authorize communication between registered agents. Authentication alone
does not establish authorization under the receiver's
policy~\cite{ellison1999spki}.

We identify an order-dependent inconsistency in the production matcher.
Under the documented most-specific semantics, the specific deny should be
selected over the wildcard allow. Instead, the matcher returns the later wildcard allowance;
reversing the rule order returns denial.

In controlled executions, the implementation decision propagates through
production consumer paths to token issuance and storage. A separate symbolic
model analyzes acceptance under the supplied divergence.

Our contributions are (1) identifying and explaining the matcher inconsistency,
and (2) characterizing its consequences through controlled production-consumer
propagation and conditional symbolic analysis.

\paragraph{Related work.}
\label{sec:related-work}
Prior work studies distributed authorization languages and policy
semantics~\cite{becker2004cassandra,becker2007secpal} and
policy verification and change-impact analysis~\cite{fisler2005margrave}.
SPKI~\cite{ellison1999spki} and Macaroons~\cite{birgisson2014macaroons}
provide related abstractions for credential- and capability-based authorization.
Symbolic protocol analyzers ProVerif and Tamarin support protocol-level
security verification~\cite{blanchet2016proverif,meier2013tamarin};
SAGA uses ProVerif to analyze its communication protocol~\cite{syros2025saga}.
Security evaluation for autonomous LLM agents has also received recent
attention~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo}.
We instead examine a concrete deviation between SAGA's specified rule-selection
semantics and its implementation, and trace its downstream consumer consequences;
this does not refute SAGA's guarantees under its model assumptions.
