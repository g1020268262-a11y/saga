\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

SAGA~\cite{syros2025saga} uses contact policies and access-control tokens
to authorize communication between registered agents. Authentication
establishes identity but does not imply permission under the receiver's
contact policy.

We identify an order-dependent inconsistency in the production matcher.
Under the documented most-specific semantics, the specific deny should be
selected over the wildcard allow. Instead, the matcher returns the later wildcard allowance;
reversing the rule order returns denial.

In controlled executions, the implementation decision propagates through
production consumer paths to token issuance and storage. A separate symbolic
model analyzes acceptance under the supplied divergence.

Our contributions are (1) identifying and explaining the matcher inconsistency,
and (2) characterizing its consequences through controlled production-consumer
propagation and conditional symbolic analysis.

\paragraph{Related work.}
\label{sec:related-work}
Prior work covers distributed authorization semantics
~\cite{becker2004cassandra,becker2007secpal}, authorization credentials
~\cite{birgisson2014macaroons}, and policy change-impact analysis
~\cite{fisler2005margrave}. ProVerif and Tamarin support symbolic protocol
analysis~\cite{blanchet2016proverif,meier2013tamarin}; SAGA uses ProVerif
~\cite{syros2025saga}. We analyze the concrete rule-selection deviation and
downstream consumer effects, complementing SAGA's protocol-level guarantees
under its assumptions.
