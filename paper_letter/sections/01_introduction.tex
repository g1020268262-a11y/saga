\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

SAGA~\cite{syros2025saga} governs communication between registered agents
using contact policies, identity checks, and access-control tokens.
Authentication establishes who is communicating; it does not establish
whether that identity is authorized to make contact.

Direct matcher calls reveal an order-dependent disagreement with the
documented most-specific rule semantics. We analyze its conditional
consequence in a fixed symbolic scenario, where ProVerif reports
\texttt{ChatAccept} reachable and its correspondence to prior
\texttt{TokenIssue} true. Separately, controlled experiments execute the
production authorization consumers through token issuance and storage,
stopping before message acceptance.

Our contributions are (1) analysis of the recorded matcher inconsistency,
(2) consumer-side propagation validation with A/A, D/D, and D/A controls,
and (3) conditional formal analysis of reachability and prior issuance.

\paragraph{Related work.}
\label{sec:related-work}
Authorization-policy work studies policy semantics and
analysis~\cite{becker2007secpal,fisler2005margrave}; ProVerif supports
symbolic protocol analysis~\cite{blanchet2016proverif}. We connect a concrete
matcher disagreement to a fixed symbolic consequence and production consumers.
