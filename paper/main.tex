\begin{abstract}
Policy-governed agent communication requires authorization decisions to
preserve intended policy semantics across evaluation and enforcement.
Authentication and confidentiality alone do not establish this consistency.
We investigate the requirement through a SAGA case study. Archived direct
calls to the unmodified contact-policy matcher demonstrate an order-dependent
authorization decision inconsistency: a specific-deny/general-allow rulebook
yields an allow-side budget although documented most-specific semantics
select denial. Separately, a diagnostic extension retains the queried
authentication correspondences and token secrecy while an added authorization
correspondence is false. This identifies a verification-coverage gap, not a
formal proof of the matcher inconsistency.
For the recorded policy input, an independent finite specification evaluator
derives Deny, while the archived matcher observation supplies Allow. A
decision bridge checks the corresponding recorded policy input and observation
provenance, constructs the decision pair, and reconstructs the fixed
authorization-divergence scenario analyzed in the archived ProVerif~2.05 run.
Under its explicit symbolic assumptions, the derived model admits
application-message acceptance (\texttt{ChatAccept}) and establishes that
acceptance implies prior token issuance (\texttt{TokenIssue}). Independent
gate controls distinguish Provider and receiver blocking stages. These
results connect implementation observations to symbolic consequences through
checked evidence transfer for the recorded case. They establish neither
cryptographic compromise nor deployed-service reproduction. Mapping the
concrete evaluation to symbolic participants remains an explicit abstraction;
general implementation-to-model refinement and correctness for arbitrary
policies remain unproved.
\end{abstract}
