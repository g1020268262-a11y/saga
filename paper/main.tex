\title{Authorization Decision Consistency in SAGA: Implementation Evidence and Formal Consequence Analysis}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Agentic systems rely on policy-governed communication, where authorization
decisions connect user policies and agent interactions. By themselves,
authentication and confidentiality do not establish consistency among policy semantics,
policy evaluation implementation, and enforcement actions. We investigate
this requirement through a SAGA case study. Implementation-level analysis
identifies an order-dependent authorization decision inconsistency in the
studied contact policy matcher: a general permission can override a more
specific denial. We use ProVerif to analyze protocol-level consequences under
an explicit abstraction of this divergence and compare Provider and receiver
authorization gates. Our evaluation combines archived matcher observations
and symbolic verification results while distinguishing their evidentiary
scope. In the diagnostic extension, the queried authentication correspondences
and token secrecy remain satisfied while an added authorization correspondence
does not hold. Separately, the consequence model admits message acceptance
under assumed policy denial and implementation permission, while retaining
correspondence to prior token issuance. These findings concern authorization
enforcement without demonstrating a cryptographic compromise. The formal
model abstracts the matcher result; it neither executes Python nor establishes
an implementation-to-model refinement. A strict semantic mapping and
production-service reproduction remain outside the completed evidence.
\end{abstract}
