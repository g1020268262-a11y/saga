\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Policy-governed agent communication requires authorization decisions to remain
consistent with policy semantics, evaluation implementation, and enforcement
actions. Authentication and confidentiality provide complementary guarantees;
establishing them does not discharge the separate obligation to justify
communication under the receiving user's policy. Our SAGA case study examines
this connection between policy decisions and the protocol actions that consume
them.

We identify an order-dependent authorization decision inconsistency in the
studied contact policy matcher through implementation-level analysis. We then
use symbolic verification to analyze the protocol consequence of an abstract
authorization divergence, including message acceptance and the distinct
blocking stages of Provider and receiver gates. The evaluation combines
archived implementation observations and formal results while preserving
their boundaries: direct matcher calls demonstrate the local inconsistency,
whereas the formal model characterizes consequences under explicit assumptions.
These evidence layers do not constitute a single executable service reproduction.

The findings demonstrate that authentication and secrecy properties can remain
valid while authorization consistency obligations are not established.
Specifically, the Stage~1 diagnostic preserves the queried authentication
correspondences and token secrecy without establishing the added authorization
correspondence. Separately, the consequence model permits message acceptance
under assumed policy denial and implementation permission, while preserving
the correspondence to prior token issuance. This distinction between credential
provenance and policy permission characterizes an authorization enforcement
gap, not a cryptographic failure.

Matcher-to-formal semantic integration remains open: the current model abstracts
the implementation result rather than deriving it from concrete policy
evaluation. Future work should establish that connection, extend modeling to
the complete deployment lifecycle, and examine additional agent systems.
These extensions are needed to assess applicability beyond the studied SAGA
implementation and symbolic scenarios.
