\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Policy-governed agent communication requires authorization decisions to remain
consistent with intended policy semantics, their implementation evaluation,
and the actions that consume them. Authentication and confidentiality provide
complementary guarantees without discharging this separate obligation. Our
SAGA case study examines the evidence connecting policy decisions to agent
communication authorization.

Archived function-level calls to the unmodified matcher identify an
order-dependent authorization decision inconsistency. For the recorded
specific-deny/general-allow rulebook, an independent finite evaluator derives
Deny while the implementation observation supplies Allow. The decision bridge
binds these values to the same concrete policy evaluation and reconstructs
the executed symbolic scenario from the checked pair. Recorded decision-value
transfer is therefore mechanized, while implementation observation and symbolic
execution retain distinct evidentiary roles.

The Stage~1 diagnostic preserves the queried authentication correspondences
and token secrecy without establishing the added authorization correspondence.
Separately, a fresh ProVerif~2.05 execution of the bridge-derived model reports
\texttt{ChatAccept} reachable and establishes that acceptance implies prior
\texttt{TokenIssue}. Independent gate controls locate distinct Provider and
receiver blocking stages. These results do not transfer Stage~1 guarantees
to the derived model or add a standalone issuance-reachability query to it.

The case study demonstrates why authentication and credential provenance
cannot substitute for authorization-decision correctness. It combines
implementation evidence, checked evidence transfer, and symbolic consequence
analysis without establishing cryptographic compromise or reproducing a
deployed-service exploit. The recorded policy evaluation and symbolic witness
are not evidence of the same concrete service session.

The remaining work concerns stronger correspondence, rather than an entirely
missing decision-transfer mechanism. Evaluator correctness is not theorem-proved;
the supported grammar does not cover complete Python or \texttt{fnmatch}
semantics or arbitrary policies. Symbolic role binding remains an abstraction,
and deployment-role correspondence and general implementation refinement remain
unproved. Future work may extend and verify the evaluator, relate symbolic
consumers to deployed components, reproduce service behavior, model the complete
deployment lifecycle, and examine additional agent systems.
