\section{Formalization}

\subsection{Limitations of the Original Model}

The registered initiating agent in Chapter~3 should not be silently identified
with the attacker in the original symbolic model. That model provides an
active network attacker over public communication and uses idealized
cryptography, while its fixed peer processes execute their prescribed roles.
Its authentication and token-secrecy queries do not include a contact rulebook
or link a policy decision to credential use.

The Stage-1 authorization diagnostic preserves those assumptions and adds
policy events without making policy evaluation an execution guard. In that
diagnostic, a deny event is recorded but does not block the protocol, and the
allow event is declared but never executed. The recorded failure of its added
correspondence therefore identifies a missing modeled authorization
dependence while the baseline authentication and secrecy results are retained.
It does not by itself establish a matcher-dependent execution by a malicious
registered agent in the implementation.

In particular, the diagnostic event named $\mathsf{Accept}$ denotes the
initiating peer's successful receipt and verification of a token. It does not
denote the receiving peer's acceptance of an application message. Its failed
correspondence is thus evidence about authorization coverage at token receipt,
not a violation of Chapter~3's message-level condition. A message-level claim
requires an event at the receiving agent's message-acceptance point and a model
connecting policy evaluation, credential issuance, and that event.
